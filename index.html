What we do

Four services, one aim: reliable water and power in the hands of young people who can run them.

Borehole services

Siting, drilling, pump installation and maintenance so households, schools and farms have dependable water.

Rural electrification

Solar systems that power pumps, homes and small businesses away from the grid, without diesel costs.

Youth training

Hands-on skills in solar installation, borehole upkeep and, now, irrigated farming and selling produce.

Youth employment

Paid roles on our projects and farm, and a route for young growers to run their own plots.

Our new solar-irrigated farm

Most small growers in Zimbabwe are held back by water. Water is what we already deliver. Our pilot farm turns that strength into fresh produce for Harare buyers and steady work for young people.

2 hectarespilot area, drip-irrigated
Solar pumpingborehole water without fuel bills
2–3 cropschosen from tomatoes, onions and potatoes
Buyers firstprice, grade and delivery agreed before planting

The first 12 months

  1. Months 1–3
    Validate.

    Confirm site, water tests, crops and buyers. Recruit the first youth group.

  2. Months 4–6
    Produce.

    Install solar drip, train on the farm, plant in stages and record every cost.

  3. Months 7–9
    Sell.

    Harvest, grade, deliver and get paid. Compare real margins with the plan.

  4. Months 10–12
    Expand.

    Grow the crops and buyers that worked. Prepare out-grower plots for year two.

Our targets

4paid farm jobs in year one
20young people trained on the farm
10youth out-growers in year two

These are targets for the pilot, not results yet. At least half of the young people we recruit will be young women.
