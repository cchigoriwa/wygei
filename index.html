The pilot farm

Most small growers in Zimbabwe are held back by water. Water is what we already deliver. Our first two hectares turn that strength into fresh produce and steady work.

The first 12 months

  1. Months 1–3
    Validate.

    Confirm site, water tests, crop mix and buyers. Recruit the first youth group.

  2. Months 4–6
    Produce.

    Install solar drip, train on the farm, plant in staggered batches and record every cost.

  3. Months 7–9
    Sell.

    Harvest, grade, deliver and get paid. Compare real margins with the plan.

  4. Months 10–12
    Expand.

    Grow the crops and buyers that worked. Prepare out-grower plots for year two.

Production and sales plan

Year-one pilot: 20 tonnes of saleable produce and US$20,000 in sales, with the farm breaking even at about two-thirds of that volume.

Target buyers and supply terms

  • Traders

    Wholesale traders at Mbare Musika for volume and quick payment.

  • Retail

    Supermarkets and greengrocers needing consistent grade and packing.

  • Food service

    Restaurants and caterers buying weekly, by standing order.

  • Institutions

    Schools, clinics and canteens with steady, predictable demand.

Before each planting we agree price per kilogram, grade, packaging, delivery days and payment terms in writing, and no single buyer takes more than 40% of sales.

These are planning assumptions for the pilot, not results. Yields are set conservatively and will be replaced with buyer prices and our own harvest records. Funding of US$10,000 puts this plan in the ground: see how it would be used.

Our edge: water and energy we already deliver

The same services that power our farm are available to households, schools, farms and communities.

Borehole services

Siting, drilling, pump installation and maintenance so households, schools and farms have dependable water.

Rural electrification

Solar systems that power pumps, homes and small businesses away from the grid, without diesel costs.

Youth training

Hands-on skills in solar installation, borehole upkeep and, now, irrigated farming and selling produce.

Youth employment

Paid roles on our projects and farm, and a route for young growers to run their own plots.
